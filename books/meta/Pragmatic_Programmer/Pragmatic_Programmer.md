# Pragmatic Programmer notes

## You are what you do

- To build something meaningful, you need to like the work and be passionate about it.
- Working only for money is a fast track to burnout — define your own motivation.
- If you like the work, you'll naturally invest more time, attention, and emotion into it, and that pays off long-term. Emotion also drives learning: once something feels important, the brain works harder to remember it.
- It's your life. Only you can figure out what to do with it — and the only way is to just start doing something.

## Broken windows

A building with one broken window, left unfixed, tends to get more broken windows over time — eventually it looks abandoned and nobody bothers maintaining it.

- Do it right the first time.
- Fix problems immediately, or at least flag them — don't let the list of "known issues" grow too large, or the whole thing starts to feel hopeless.
- Keep the environment in good shape. A good environment keeps you in a good mood, and a good mood makes you more likely to keep doing good work.

## Stone soup

Hungry soldiers pass through a starving village asking for food. Villagers have nothing to spare, so the soldiers boil water with stones, claiming they're making "stone soup." Curious villagers ask about it, and the soldiers casually mention it'd be even better with a carrot, then an onion, and so on. Villagers keep contributing ingredients out of curiosity, and eventually everyone shares a real meal.

- Ask for one small thing at a time.
- Stay curious — it draws people in.
- Build alliances; together you have more resources than alone.

## Boiled frog

Drop a frog into hot water and it jumps out immediately. Put it in cold water that's heated slowly, and it won't notice the change until it's too late.

- Change takes time — keep working, and the results will show eventually.
- Pay attention to what's changing around you, even when it's gradual.

## Good enough is better than perfect (and perfect can be the enemy of good)

When working on a product or idea, it's tempting to aim for the ideal version right away. It's better to ship a good-enough version first, gather feedback, and iterate toward something better.

## Knowledge portfolio

Investing in your own knowledge always pays the best return.
