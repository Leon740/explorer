# Just Do It — But First, Stop Overfitting

There's an old saying: once a cat jumps on a hot stove, it will never jump on a stove again — even a cold one.

Humans do something similar. We overgeneralize from limited data. When we draw a conclusion from a single data point, we still leave room for doubt. But once we've seen the same thing happen a few times, we start treating it as 100% true.

Take the job market. Everyone says it's brutal right now, and that dating apps don't work. Once you notice other people struggling with the same problem, it's tempting to treat your problem as _global_ rather than _local_ — and that's dangerous, because it becomes an excuse to give up. "Why bother, it's broken for everyone."

I'm not saying global trends don't exist — they do. But you never really know why other people are struggling. Maybe they're doing something wrong and just don't tell you about it.

My approach: treat every problem as local — as yours to solve. As Roosevelt put it: "Do what you can, with what you have, where you are."

Struggling with your job search doesn't mean you should give up — it might mean it's time to try a different approach: rewrite your resume, ask others to review it, change your strategy. Same with sports: bad sessions happen, sometimes even bad weeks. Maybe you're overtrained and don't realize it. Either way, it's on you to notice, adjust, and keep going.
